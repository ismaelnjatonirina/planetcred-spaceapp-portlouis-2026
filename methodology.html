Satellite methodology

Data cleaning

A clear sky is not found by filtering only scenes with a low overall cloud percentage. The pipeline masks invalid pixels, measures clear coverage over Chamarel, then combines valid pixels from multiple acquisitions.

1. Landsat quality mask

In QA_PIXEL, remove bits 0–5: fill, dilated cloud, cirrus, cloud, cloud shadow, and snow/ice. In QA_RADSAT, remove saturated red/NIR pixels and terrain-occluded pixels.

2. Local clear coverage

Measure the share of valid surface remaining in the project area after masking. The script keeps an observation only when at least 60% of the area is usable. This local check is more useful than CLOUD_COVER, which summarizes the entire scene.

3. Before / after composites

For display, the script takes the median of clear pixels over annual periods (2015 and 2025 to the present). Persistent clouds remain masked instead of being replaced with invented values. The generated files are landsat_before.png and landsat_after.png.

4. Resolution: what zoom can do

The current VIIRS GIBS layer is 250 m per pixel. For the project area, the script creates a 10 m Sentinel‑2 composite and a downloadable RGB GeoTIFF. This shows plots and forest cover more clearly, but not individual trees. Metre-scale Google Earth imagery requires authorized aerial or commercial data.

Other constraints to check

Thin clouds, haze, terrain shadows, saturated pixels, water, small sample sizes, and seasonality can bias NDVI. Compare the same season, measure clear-pixel coverage for each observation, record selected dates, and do not interpolate large gaps. Sentinel‑2 is used here for 10 m visual imagery; the NDVI trend remains based on Landsat 8 at 30 m, and the two series are not mixed.
